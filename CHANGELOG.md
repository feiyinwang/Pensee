# Changelog

## 2026-10-27

- The headings of a note are now one thin line down the right edge, cut into a piece per heading, each as long as its section, so you can see at a glance which parts are long. The heading you're reading is lit, with a dot where you are in it. Hover the line and the headings open beside it, each level with its piece; click one to go there. It's the same line at every window width: the **On this page** list no longer stands beside the page.
- A talk can go on after you stop. Press **Continue** and the next take goes under the last one's transcript, in the same talk, and its topics carry on where they were. Every take you've recorded is in **Recordings** in the sidebar: pick one to hear it, read its transcript and open the note it's in. In Settings → Transcription you choose who writes the transcript: Whisper on your Mac (Small, or Large v3 Turbo, which hears accents and quiet rooms better), or ElevenLabs Scribe with your own API key, which stays in your keychain.
- While you record, the pill draws your voice as it comes in: a red trace that rises when you talk and goes flat when you stop. If you go to another note, recording carries on and the pill comes with you, saying which note it's for, with **Stop** at hand. Close the note and the take stops and is saved to Recordings, so the microphone is never left on. The pill and the topics box now fit a column when you pinch to see your tabs side by side, and Record no longer hides under the word count at the bottom. If your Mac isn't letting Pensée hear the microphone, the pill says so and where to allow it, instead of recording silence. The topics now use your model from Settings → Agent (at least Sonnet) and redraw when you say where something goes ("put the ads test under Rob's skills"), keeping each branch to one idea. If you stop a talk while you're on another note, the talk, its transcript and its topics still go into the note you recorded in. The topic tabs are plain tabs now, not a pill inside a pill.
- A topic's mind map or flowchart is now drawn as one of your boards, in the board's own style, and saved as a board beside the note. Click it while you talk to open it in the board editor: move a branch, rename a topic, add your own cards. What you change by hand stays: the AI keeps drawing around it and never moves it back, and what you delete stays gone. When you stop, the board is in the note with a sentence or two above it on what you concluded, instead of a list of everything you said. Each topic on the board has a small play mark: click it to hear just the part of the talk it came from. Charts stay charts.
- Brainstorms are drawn as a mind map with coloured branches, even a plan told as "first…, then…". Arrange it right in the talk box: drag a topic onto another to put it under it, or onto a neighbour's edge to put it before or after, and it stays where you put it. Say "as steps", "as a matrix", "as a timeline" or "as a pie" to change a topic's picture while you talk; steps are drawn as the board's blue cards. A topic that changed picture now goes into the note as the picture you ended with, and after Stop the note shows its topics straight away.

## 2026-10-26

- While you record, Pensée sorts what you say into topics. A box above **Record** shows a tab for each topic with a diagram that grows as you talk: a chart for numbers, a map for options, a mind map for reasons and doubts. When you turn to something new ("okay, next…"), it asks **New topic?** before opening a tab, and you can keep it as an aside instead; carry on talking about it and it opens the tab for you. When you stop, each topic goes under the transcript with its diagram and what you said, each line with its time. The diagrams stay plain Mermaid text you can edit. Topics need Whisper on your Mac and Claude Code signed in.
- A talk you record is now written out for you, on your Mac. After **Stop**, Pensée downloads Whisper Small once (488 MB, the model Superwhisper's free plan uses) and transcribes the take without sending it anywhere. The transcript goes under the player, folded; open it to read every line with its time, and click a time to hear that moment. Notes with Obsidian callouts (`> [!note]`, `> [!tip]-`) now show them with a header you can fold.
- You can record a talk in a note. Press **Record** at the bottom of the note and talk; **Stop** saves the audio in a `Recordings` folder in your vault and adds a `Talk` heading with a player under the line you were on. The first time, your Mac asks to use the microphone. Play, pause and drag along the bar to listen back.
- Diagrams in your notes look new. A ` ```mermaid ` block is now drawn by Pele, which reads all 32 kinds of Mermaid diagram, in the same style as Pensée's other charts: soft shapes with a thin outline, colours by group, quiet labels. Pies, bar and line charts, Sankey flows, radar charts, treemaps, file trees and kanban boards become live charts you can point at for their numbers. The text stays plain Mermaid, so the note opens the same anywhere. A diagram with a mistake says what's wrong.
- On Windows, Pensée finds Claude Code even when it was installed after Pensée or by npm or WinGet, so onboarding no longer says it's missing. Ctrl+, opens Preferences, the first launch fills the screen, and Settings and onboarding say Ctrl and touchpad, not Cmd and trackpad.
- Pensée now builds for Windows too, from the same code as the Mac app. Windows has its own title bar, uses Ctrl where the Mac uses ⌘ (labels and tooltips say Ctrl+O, Ctrl+Shift+Z), and opens Obsidian vaults and finds Claude Code where Windows keeps them. Apple Books import and window translucency stay Mac-only. Each release now comes with a Windows installer next to the Mac download. On the Mac, nothing changes.

## 2026-10-24

- A new project now starts empty. If your vault has a Templater project template, a project made in Pensée used to come with its headings, Dataview queries and properties, shown as raw code. Now it holds only its name and status, whether you add it from Projects or with **Add to Projects** in a note. It still goes in your projects folder.
- What's new now only comes with an update to Pensée. It used to show changes that weren't in your version yet. Now it shows what changed in the version you have, once, after you update. If you skipped a few updates, it shows every day since you last looked in one card, titled with the days it covers ("October 16–18"). **Read all** opens the whole list in your browser, at the day the card showed.
- You can drop an HTML file into a note. Drag a `.html` page from Finder onto the writing area and it lands where you let go as an embed (`![[chart.html]]`), framed and running, the same as picking it with `/html`. Before, only pictures could be dropped, and an HTML file did nothing. The file stays where it is, so its own styles and data still load; a file under your workspace is linked from the workspace, one from elsewhere relative to the note.
- A picture or page dropped from Finder now lands where you let go. On a Retina screen the app read the drop at half its position, so a picture went in higher up and further left than you aimed, and a drop near the sidebar or the top of the note missed the note and did nothing.
- An embedded HTML page can grow to fill its note. Press **Expand** in the page's header and the page grows over the note right where it is, without reloading, so a chart or a guide gets the whole width and height. **Shrink back**, or Esc, puts it back in its place in the text. Nothing in the note changes.

## 2026-10-21

- The keys that control a video now only work while your pointer is on it. ← and → skip it, and ⇧, and ⇧. (`<` `>`) change its speed, only with the pointer over the video; anywhere else ← and → move the caret and `<` `>` type, in the video's own note too. Clicking the video and moving away no longer leaves the arrows skipping it. The same goes for a podcast's player. ⌘S still plays and pins from anywhere in the note.
- A note you open with ⌘O now comes into view. With several tabs side by side, if you had scrolled away from the one you were in, the note used to open in that tab off screen and nothing moved. Now the row of tabs slides over to it. The same goes for a note picked from the sidebar, and for Back and Forward.
- ⌘O takes you to a note that's already open. Press ⌘O and Enter on a recent note, and if a tab already shows it, the row of tabs slides over to that tab instead of opening a second copy where you are. If it's the note you're in, the row slides back to it.
- Adding something with ⌘N now takes you to it. Paste a YouTube link, press ⌘↵, and its note opens, instead of a notice at the top asking whether to open it. To add it and keep your place, press ⌘⇧↵, or click the arrow beside the button and pick **… and stay here**; the menu lists both ways with their keys. The same goes for a plain note.
- A YouTube link with a time in it now plays from that time, as it does on YouTube. Paste `https://youtu.be/…?t=210` and the video in its note starts at 3:30. Times written `t=3m30s` or `t=1h2m3s`, and an embed link's `start=`, work too. If the video already has a note, pasting the link in ⌘N or ⌘O opens that note and plays from the link's time.
- Picking a page while typing a `[[link]]` in a bullet works again. With a suggestion highlighted, Enter now puts the whole link down, `[[Job-to-be-done]]`, instead of starting a new bullet and leaving `[[job-` behind. The same goes for `@today` and the `/` menu in a list.
- A video's timeline is easier to use. Point at it and the line thickens, the chapter under the pointer thickens more, and a knob shows where the video is, so it's clear you can click or drag. The tooltip names the whole chapter and the time there, and the controls stay up while your pointer rests on them, so they no longer vanish while you read. With the pointer over the video, ← and → skip it 5 seconds back or on, as on YouTube, instead of moving the caret in your note.

## 2026-10-19

- A line you highlighted in a book now ends in a pin in the book's note, the same pin a video note's moments have, so you can tell it came from the book. Hover it to see where: the page, or the chapter for an EPUB. Click it and the book scrolls to the passage and flashes it, opening the book first if it's closed. The pin is the highlight: Backspace after it removes the pin and the highlight with it.
- Pensée now tells you what changed. When Pensée's changelog has a new day, a card shows once in the bottom-right corner. It has a painting of someone reading or writing (Vermeer, Rembrandt, Hammershøi and others, a different one each update), the day's changes in a sentence each, **N more** for the rest, and **Read all** for the whole changelog on GitHub. **Got it** puts it away until the next update, and ⌘P → **Show What's New** brings it back. A first launch shows onboarding, not a card.

## 2026-10-18

- Pensée now welcomes you on first launch. It asks one thing per screen: which notes to open (your Obsidian vaults are listed with their note counts; ↵ opens the first), which AI to use (Claude Code, signed in from right there, or the line to install it), and something to try it on. Pick **YouTube**, **Podcast**, **Book** or **Just write** and it opens as a real note in a `Pensée samples` folder, with a guide at the bottom for one loop. On the video, Huberman Lab's dopamine episode opens at the motivation tool: click the line and Pensée writes what stuck, ⌘S pins it to the second, then ⌘. `@` checks it against real papers. The book is _The Elements of Style_ (public domain), downloaded for you. Then a carousel shows the rest in short animated demos with their keys: pinching between one tab and all of them, docking cards on a board, Connect, Check, Organize, a book beside its note, and pinned podcast lines. Swipe it with two fingers, or use ← →. If you already use Pensée, nothing changes; ⌘P → **Show Onboarding** plays it any time.
- Pensée can now be installed with one line: `brew install --cask feiyinwang/tap/pensee`, or the `curl … install.sh | bash` line in the README for Macs without Homebrew. Both fetch the latest release (0.6.0, Apple Silicon) from GitHub. `scripts/release.sh` publishes the next one.
- Adding a paper from a link now brings in its PDF. Press ⌘N and paste a link to a PDF, or to a paper's page on arXiv, OpenReview or a journal that offers its PDF: Pensée saves the PDF beside the note, embeds it at the top, and with ⌘⇧↵ opens it in the reader next to the note. The note stays a Paper. A link that ends in `.pdf` is now guessed as a paper. If the PDF needs a sign-in, the note is still made and Pensée says it couldn't get the PDF.
- Clicking a paper in Library now opens its PDF in the reader beside its note, as a book does. It reads the first PDF the note links to, like `![[paper.pdf]]`, so papers you brought from Obsidian work as they are.
- The **Bookmarks** tab in Library and the X sync behind it are gone for now, until they're ready. Settings no longer has **X Bookmarks**, Pensée no longer checks X in the background, and a bookmark note opens as a plain note. Bookmark notes already in your vault stay where they are.
- When **Add to Projects** finds a line that looks like a project you already have, it now asks first. **Already a project?** shows your line and that project as a card, with its status and the start of its note: **Same project** adds nothing, **New project** makes its own. With several, it asks about each in turn in the same popup. Esc or a click outside adds nothing new. Projects in **Done** don't count: a line like a finished project becomes a new project.
- **Add to Projects** now keeps each bullet you select as its own project. Three bullets for a website, a Discord channel and a support email make three projects, not one. A bullet is judged by the work it takes, not by how short it is, so "Make a Discord channel" counts. Bullets only share a project when one is a step of another, like booking flights under a trip. Projects you already have are recognised even when worded differently, so adding the same lines twice no longer makes a second copy.
- Pinching between one tab and all your tabs side by side no longer slows down the more tabs you have open. Each switch used to restyle and lay out every open tab, hidden ones included; now it only lays out the tabs whose layout changes. With 20 tabs open a switch takes about a third of the time it did (14ms instead of 46ms). Each tab keeps its scroll place. With a book open, a switch now takes about a fifth of the time it did (about 50ms instead of 245ms in a window with 36 tabs): the book stays laid out while its tab is hidden instead of laying out the whole book again each time, and clicks while it's hidden stay fast.
- Pinching between one tab and all your tabs side by side now switches as soon as your fingers start to move, about a quarter of the pinch it took before, and you can pinch back the other way without lifting your fingers to switch back. With a book open, each switch took about half a second; now it lands in the next frame. Nothing flashes as it switches: islands no longer fade in a dark outline, a book's column no longer shows blank for a moment, and the H1–H6 labels, block handles, list guides and **On this page** sit in their new places in the first frame instead of jumping there after it.
- A note Pensée makes (from **Add to Projects**, **Add project** on the board, or anywhere else) now shows right away on the Projects board, in your spaces and in search. Before, it only turned up after you quit and reopened Pensée.

## 2026-10-17

- Select sentences in any note, right-click and pick **Add to Projects**, the first item in the menu whenever text is selected (also for text selected in a video transcript or a thread). Claude reads them and makes a project for each one that takes more than two steps: a sentence per project when each is its own plan, one project when they all describe the same thing. A name you already wrote (**Garden**:, [[Trip]], "Website redesign: …") is kept; otherwise the project gets a short name close to what you wrote. One-step things like "Call Sam" are left out, and the notice says how many. Every project lands in **No status** on the board. Each new project note starts with your sentences; a note with that name already in your vault is used instead of a duplicate. **Open Projects** in the notice takes you to the board.
- ⌘↓ now takes you to the very bottom of a note and ⌘↑ to the very top, title included. Add ⇧ to select up to there.
- Select several list items, hover any of them and pick **Numbered list** (or Bulleted, To-do, a heading…) from its handle: every selected item changes, not just that one, and the numbers run 1, 2, 3 on from the item above. Items nested inside stay nested. Pick the lit tile again to turn them all back to text.
- A list item's handle now takes the items nested under it along: **Delete**, **Cut**, **Copy** and **Duplicate** act on the item with everything inside it, instead of leaving the nested items stranded under the item above. **Duplicate** and **Insert below** put the new item after the nested ones, not between the item and them. In a numbered list the new item gets the next number and the items after it move up one (a deleted item closes the gap), and a duplicate no longer copies the item's block-link id. With several items selected, every action in the handle takes all of them: **Move up/down** moves them as a group, **Duplicate** copies the group below itself, **Delete**, **Cut** and **Copy** take them all, and one ⌘Z undoes it. **Copy link to block** hides while several items are selected, since a link points at one block. A **Delete** from the handle right after you type is now its own ⌘Z: it no longer undoes your typing along with it.
- **On this page** now marks the section filling most of your screen: a heading counts as the one you're reading once it passes the middle of the screen, not only once it reaches the top. At the end of a note the last heading in view is marked, and a heading you click in the list stays marked until you scroll or type, even when its section is short.
- **# Organize** is quicker: it writes about half as much as before, so a short note is organized in about 5 seconds instead of 7 or 8, and longer notes save more. Each point the pen writes now only says what your sentences don't (what they share, what they show); where it would just repeat one of yours, it leaves the point out and your sentence sits straight under the point above, so the outline is shorter to read too.
- The pen's box (⌘.) now always opens where you can see it. At the end of a long paragraph, or with lines selected up from the bottom of a long note, it used to open at the top, out of view; now it opens just above the caret.
- **# Organize** no longer pours every topic into the same five parts. Each topic is now a tree grown from how your writing hangs together: a reason with its examples under it, the steps of something you described, a feeling and what set it off. Flat notes give a shallow tree, ideas that nest give a deeper one (four levels at most), and parts your writing doesn't support, like Rules or Open questions, are left out. Every line the pen writes still starts with a bold label for what kind of line it is (**Theory:**, **Reason:**, **Example:**…), and your own words, unchanged, now sit right under the point they back instead of all together under **Evidence** at the end.

## 2026-10-16

- In a video's transcript, **Back to now** now shows for any scroll that leaves the line being said, not only a wheel, a touch or a press on the scrollbar. Click it and the transcript follows the video again.
- In a video note, **On this page** now sits under the video and its transcript, right of your notes, and moves up with them as they pin to the top. It no longer covers the transcript, so you can scroll the transcript again.
- The video and podcast players now show their keys in full instead of Play and Pin buttons: **Play ⌘S** while paused (**Pin ⌘S** while playing), **Slower ⇧,** and **Faster ⇧.**. Under a video they start the row, before **Transcript**; under a podcast they sit below the card. ⇧, and ⇧. also change the speed now with Pinyin or another input source that types 《 》 on those keys; before, they did nothing there.
- A podcast's player now names its keys beside **Pin**, as a video's row does: **Speed < >**, and **Play ⌘S** while it's paused. While it plays, ⌘S pins, so the key moves onto **Pin ⌘S**. The speed button just shows the speed now.
- When your window leaves room to the right of the page, a note's headings now stand listed there under **On this page**, indented by level, with a short bar marking the one you're reading. Click one to jump to it. When the room runs out (a narrower window, a wider page, notes side by side), it folds back into the small ticks you hover to see the list.
- When Apple Books highlights need Full Disk Access, Pensée now shows you where to turn it on instead of a one-line notice. A real screenshot of System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access plays the steps in order: it opens on the page's title, scrolls to the end of the list, clicks **+**, Pensée joins the list, and a cursor turns it on, with numbered markers on **+**, Pensée and its switch matching the three steps beside them. It also says plainly that Pensée reads your highlights on this Mac and uploads nothing. **Open Settings** goes straight to that page; come back and the guide says _Full Disk Access is on_ and imports your highlights. It opens by itself at launch or when you turn **Import Highlights** on, without taking your typing away from the note. With **Reduce motion** on, the picture shows the last frame.
- Shortcuts are simpler to remember. Five do most of the work, each ⌘ and the first letter of what it does: ⌘N new note, ⌘T today, ⌘O open a note, ⌘P everything else, and ⌘S in a video or podcast note. Projects, Library and Files now open with ⌘G and then P, L or F (⌘G T is Today), instead of ⌥P, ⌥L and ⌥F: press ⌘G and each place in the sidebar shows its letter in a small box (with the sidebar hidden, a bar at the bottom of the window names them). It works while you write, and the letter never lands in your note. ⌘1 to ⌘9 still switch tabs. In the find bar, ↵ goes to the next match (⌘G still does there too). ⌘O now finds notes only and shows your recent ones before you type; ⌘P lists only commands, each with its key, as in Obsidian. In a video or podcast note, ⌘S pins the moment and pauses so you can write about it, and ⌘S again plays on; ⌘⇧J is gone. ⌘⇧N and ⌘⇧L are gone too: **New Note for Today** and **Add to Library…** are in ⌘P.
- With **Import Highlights** on in Settings → Apple Books, Pensée no longer makes macOS ask "access data from other apps" at every launch. macOS never keeps that answer, so Pensée now checks Apple Books by itself only once it has Full Disk Access (System Settings → Privacy & Security), which macOS does keep. Until then it waits and says so once, with **Open Settings**; **Import Apple Books Highlights** in the command palette still works any time.
- Board sections: double-click a section's name to rename it right there, and press Enter when done. Select a section and pick **Section colour** in the bar above it to tint it; the name and edge take the colour too. Dragging a section by its name now carries everything inside it, including cards you made before drawing the section around them.
- Pensée built on this Mac now keeps your answers to macOS privacy prompts, like "access data from other apps" for Apple Books highlights. Allow once and an update no longer asks again on first launch. Each Mac signs its builds with its own signing identity, made once in your Library; no key or password is in the repo or the app.
- Boards no longer have the **Sticky note** and **Mind map** tools, and N and M no longer pick them. Sticky notes and mind maps already on your boards stay and still edit as before.
- A podcast's player now looks like a video's: a dark card with the same rounded corners and soft shadow, in light and dark mode, instead of a pale outlined box.
- The row under a video now shows all its keys, not just **Pin ⌘S**: **Play ⌘⇧J** (it reads **Pause** while the video plays, and clicking it plays or pauses without taking you out of your note) and **Speed < >**.
- Docking a card on a board is calmer now. Drag a card near another and a faint imprint of it shows where it will land, with the arrow it will get already drawn, lit in the card's colour; the card it joins glows softly and breathes, and a small ring goes out from where the arrow leaves. Let go and the card glides into its spot, a dot runs down the new arrow and the glow passes to the card it reaches. It replaces the lightning bolt. With **Reduce motion** on, the imprint, arrow and glow show still and nothing plays on drop.
- Organize's bold labels (**Theory:**, **How it works:**, **Rules:**, **Evidence:**, a topic's name) are now the same blue as the pen's words beside them, instead of black, so everything the pen wrote reads as its own.
- After **# Organize**, the bottom bar now also says _4 topics, a theory each. Your words are under Evidence._ with **↵ keep** and **Esc put mine back**, so you can still see what to do when the line under a long outline has scrolled out of view.
- Shortcuts now match Obsidian's, so your hands don't have to relearn anything. ⌘O, ⌘P and ⌘⇧F open search (the sidebar's **Search** field shows ⌘O); ⌘F finds in the note you're writing, and ⌥⌘F opens it with **Replace** (and is a board's find). ⌘L turns a line into a task, then ticks and unticks it; ⌘D deletes the line; ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ go back and forward even while you write; ⌘⇧T reopens the tab you just closed; ⌥↵ (or ⌘↵ on a link) follows the link at the cursor; ⌘9 jumps to the last tab. ⌘⇧V is a plain paste again: **Paste as linked block** moved to ⌥⌘V. ⌘E no longer makes inline code (it is Obsidian's reading view). Projects, Library and Files open with ⌥P, ⌥L and ⌥F (shown when you hover them); on a keyboard where ⌥L types a real character, like @ on a German one, it still types it in a note.
- **# Organize** now builds understanding instead of handing your writing back. It splits what you selected into topics, and under each writes a one-sentence **Theory** (what you can now believe, beyond this one case), **How it works**, **Rules** for next time and **Open** questions, in the pen's ink. Your own words stay exactly as you wrote them under **Evidence**. At the top of a note each topic is a heading; inside a list it is a bold item.
- Settings → Agent now has a **Claude** row that says whether Claude is signed in, so you no longer find out only when Organize or the Agent fails. Signed out, **Sign in** opens the sign-in popup and your browser, as after a failed request. Signed in, **Sign out** signs Claude Code out on this Mac (Terminal too), and the next pen or Agent request offers to sign in again. The row checks again each time Settings opens and after you sign in.
- Opening Pensée now shows its app icon, the one in your Dock, on the page: the icon settles in, its ¶ is written in, PENSÉE is written under it, and the page dissolves into your notes, in about a second and a quarter. Click or press any key and it goes straight to your notes. With **Reduce motion** on in macOS, the window appears still.

## 2026-10-15

- Search is ⌘F now, everywhere (the sidebar's **Search** field shows it). Find in the note you're writing moved to ⌘⇧F, and a board's own find too. ⌘P, ⌘L and ⌘O open Projects, Library and Files instead, so ⌘O no longer opens search.
- Pinning the moment in a video or podcast note is ⌘S now (**Pin** shows ⌘S). Elsewhere ⌘S does nothing.
- Opening Pensée, the window now settles in instead of popping on: everything fades up a few pixels and comes into focus at once, in about a third of a second. You can click and type straight away. With **Reduce motion** on in macOS, it appears still, as before.
- ⌘P pins the moment in a video or podcast note: the line you're writing gets the time playing now, and **Pin** shows ⌘P. Everywhere else ⌘P is still search, and ⌘⇧P opens search from anywhere, a video note included. ⌘⇧M no longer pins.
- Courses are videos now: Library has no **Courses** tab, and notes with `Type: Course` show under **Videos**. A Coursera, Udemy or edX link added to Library is guessed as a video.
- A video note shows **Transcript** only when YouTube lists captions for the video. A video without captions, or one that couldn't be checked, has no **Transcript** button, and pasting its link no longer pops up "This video has no transcript".
- A video note's transcript now sits to the right of the video when the window has room, as tall as the video and pinned at the top with it while you scroll; in a narrower window it stays under the video. The line being said is highlighted instead of revealed word by word. Fold the box from its header (it remembers), and when the video has captions in more than one language, a menu next to **Transcript** reads it again in another. New transcripts come in English when the video has English captions.
- **# Organize** works on a single paragraph too: select one line, or as many as you like, and its points become the outline. Picking a pill keeps the pen's box as wide as its row of pills, with no empty space after the last one; its hint now reads _Add a direction, or just press Enter_, since the lit pill already names it.
- When Claude is signed out (an expired or revoked sign-in), the pen and the Agent now say _Claude is signed out. Sign in, then try again._ and a popup offers **Sign in**. It floats over the window without darkening it, so you can keep writing while it's up. Your browser opens Claude's sign-in page, and once you approve there the popup says _Signed in_ and closes. If the browser didn't open, **Open the sign-in page again**; if it shows a code instead of returning, paste it into the popup. No Terminal needed. Body text in the editor now sits at 72% and bold at 100%, so emphasis reads as brighter as well as heavier. Sidebar, menus and other UI text keep their colours.
- Make the block handle menu's hover highlight land at once. Rows and type tiles faded their fill in over 100ms on the ease-out curve, which read as lag when the pointer was already on the row. The fill now transitions in 50ms on a plain ease curve, via new `--motion-hover` and `--ease-hover` tokens in `docs/motion.md`.
- Number nested numbered lists the way a word processor does. A numbered item under item `2.` used to show only its own digits, so the nesting read `1.` under `2.` with nothing to say which list it belonged to. It now draws the numbers of the items above it in front of its own: `2.1.`, `2.2.`, and `2.2.1.` one level further down. The chain stops at a bullet, so a numbered list under `- ` still counts from `1.`. Nothing changes in the file; the extra numbers are drawn, not written, and the marker slot and hanging indent grow with the label so wrapped text stays lined up under the body. See [`SPECs/multilevel-list-numbering-spec.md`](SPECs/multilevel-list-numbering-spec.md).
- Let numbered lists nest with Tab. Tab on a `1.` line was swallowed and did nothing: the handler that finds the item to nest under only recognised bullet markers, so a numbered item never had a parent. It now sees numbered items too, and nests to the parent's content column (three spaces under `1. `, four under `10. `) instead of a fixed two, which is the indent CommonMark needs before it treats the line as a child. The digits stay in sequence as items move: a `2.` tabbed under `1.` becomes that nested list's `1.`, the `3.` below it closes the gap and becomes `2.`, and Shift+Tab reverses it. A nested numbered line also draws with the same hanging indent per level a nested bullet gets, so its wrapped text lines up under its own number.
- Zoom the window. `Cmd+=` (or `Cmd++`) zooms in, `Cmd+-` zooms out and `Cmd+0` returns to actual size, stepping along the browser ladder up to 300%; the same three actions sit in a new View menu. This is real page zoom, so the sidebar, tabs, editor and status bar all scale together and reflow at the narrower width instead of clipping, which is what WCAG 1.4.4 asks for at 200%. The level is saved as a global **Zoom** slider under Appearance, so a reader who needs 200% gets it on every launch. Inside a board the keys still zoom the canvas. See [`SPECs/window-zoom-spec.md`](SPECs/window-zoom-spec.md).
- Fix the line above jumping to heading size the moment you type `-` under it. Markdown reads any run of dashes on the line after a paragraph as an H2 underline, and an empty list item is not allowed to interrupt a paragraph, so `-` and `- ` both blew the previous line up to a heading until the first letter of the item arrived and the list finally won. A line of one or two dashes now ends the paragraph instead: `-` renders as the empty bullet it is about to become, and the prose above keeps its size. Three or more dashes still underline a heading, so hand-written `---` headings are untouched.
- Make the sidebar toggle instant. `Cmd+[`, `Cmd+]` and the toggle button were moving the sidebar edge on the 180ms spring meant for drag releases, but a resize drag lands on its final width with nothing left to settle, so the spring only ever ran for the toggle. The sidebar, its toggle button and the tab strip now snap into place with no animation.
- Edit table cells in place. Clicking a rendered table used to drop the whole block into markdown source, a wall of pipes for any table with prose in it. A click on a cell now opens an editor inside that cell, showing the cell's raw inline markdown with pipes and line breaks unescaped, while the rest of the table stays rendered. Enter commits and moves down, Tab and Shift+Tab move across cells, Tab past the last cell adds a row, Shift+Enter adds a line break, Escape discards, and clicking away commits. Only the edited cell's characters change in the file, so hand-aligned padding in the other cells survives, and one `Cmd+Z` undoes a cell. Links inside cells still navigate. Source mode is still there behind a code button in the table's corner, and keyboard travel into the table unfolds it as before. See [`SPECs/table-in-place-cell-editing-spec.md`](SPECs/table-in-place-cell-editing-spec.md).
- Paste a copied table and get a table. Anything copied from a spreadsheet, Notion, Google Docs, Confluence or a web page lands on the clipboard as tab-separated rows, which markdown reads as one run of prose per row with the columns invisibly glued together. The editor now recognises that shape on paste (two or more rows, every row with a tab) and inserts it as a pipe table with the first row as the header, rendered by the same table widget a hand-written table gets. Cells are trimmed, pipes are escaped, spreadsheet-quoted cells are unwrapped, a line break inside a cell becomes `<br>` and now renders as a break instead of literal text, and short rows are padded to the widest. The table is set off from neighbouring text with the blank lines it needs to parse. Nothing changes inside a code block, inline code or an existing table, and **Paste as plain text** in the context menu inserts the raw text, which is the first time it does something **Paste** does not. One `Cmd+Z` undoes the whole paste. See [`SPECs/paste-tabular-data-as-table-spec.md`](SPECs/paste-tabular-data-as-table-spec.md).
- Add `Cmd+[` and `Cmd+]` as shortcuts for toggling the sidebar, and move the toggle button into the sidebar's header at its right edge, next to the divider and the back/forward arrows beyond it, so it sits on the edge it controls and follows it as the sidebar resizes. When the sidebar is hidden the button waits beside the traffic lights. Inside the editor, indent less / indent more move from `Cmd+[` / `Cmd+]` to `Cmd+Shift+[` / `Cmd+Shift+]`.
- Speed up every animation. Click- and state-driven motion now uses one ease-out curve and finishes in under 180ms (the compact file picker morph drops from 260ms to 160ms, section rail ticks and popover rows from 300ms to 160ms and 100ms, the settings toggle and tree chevrons from 200ms to 120ms). The sidebar edge settles on a spring after a resize drag instead of a linear ease. Every button now presses in to 97% while held. The block handle menu closes as soon as the pointer moves away from it (a 60ms grace instead of 320ms), and dragged blocks and columns settle into place on the same spring. Tokens and rules live in `docs/motion.md`.
- Fix a heading's `#` drawing on the line below while the heading is still empty. The hanging hash is positioned out of the line's flow, so a heading with no text yet — `#` the instant you type it on a blank line — had nothing holding the line open: it collapsed to its padding and the full-size glyph hung off its bottom edge, straight over the next line's words, snapping back only once a title gave the line height. An empty heading line now holds the same height as a filled one, sized from the same table the heading text is.
- Recognise a bare `#` line as the empty heading it is. The block menu demanded a space after the hashes, so a heading left with no text became a ghost: its hash hidden, the type grid calling it Text, **Heading 1** on it writing `# #`, and Paragraph unable to clear it — while any action taken beside it bred another. Picking the lit heading tile on one now removes it, and the neighbouring line is left alone.
- Fix a new note keeping an empty `#` above the first thing you write. Every note is created from a `# ` template — an empty heading waiting for a title — and pressing Enter on it left the hashes stranded on their own line while the words went to the next one, which renders as the `#` clamped onto that line's content. Enter on a heading with no text now clears the heading instead, exactly as it already does on an empty list item. Typing the title straight into the template still makes a real heading.
- Fix the gutter grip jumping to the wrong line, so block-menu actions applied to a line you were not pointing at. A markdown paragraph is every consecutive non-blank line, so a single Enter leaves two independent lines inside one node — and the grip addressed that node, parking itself on its first line however far down you pointed. Turning the second line into a heading put the `#` on the first. For prose the grip now addresses the **line** under the pointer; lists, quotes, tables, fences and a picture with words under it stay whole, because there the lines really are one block. Move up/down follows the same units, and reordering two lines of a paragraph no longer splits them apart with a blank line.
- Fix the block menu's H1/H2/H3 tiles stamping a `#` onto the next line's content. Two lines with no blank line between them are one markdown paragraph — which is what a single Enter produces — and the conversion prefixed every line of the block, so turning one line into a heading turned the line under it into a heading too. A heading is one line: only the line the grip is parked beside gets the hashes, and whatever followed stays behind as its own block, keeping the shape it already had (a bulleted list stays bulleted, a quote stays quoted). This is the same `setBlockType` the `/h1` slash command goes through, so both paths still produce identical markdown.
- Add a daily note. The new-tab page gains a **Today's note** button next to Create new note, bound to `Cmd+Shift+N` and also available as **Open Today's Note** in the command palette. The first press of the day creates `2026-09-02.md` from the usual new-note template and drops the caret in the title; every later press reopens that same note untouched, so the shortcut is safe to hit all day. Notes land at the workspace root by default, or in the folder set by the new **Daily Note Folder** preference under Workspace (`Journal/Daily` works, and missing folders are created). The date is your local calendar date, so writing at 23:30 still files under today.
