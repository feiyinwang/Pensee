# Writer — logo brief (draft)

## The product

Writer is a local-first markdown editor for people who think in plain text: Obsidian vaults, docs repos, personal wikis. It has AI built in, but the writing stays yours.

## What we believe

AI has made answers cheap. Clear thinking hasn't got any cheaper: you still get it by writing down what you actually care about, then working it until it's true. Writer is where that work happens. It treats AI as something to think _with_, not something that thinks _for_ you.

## What the mark must say

**Thinking is made by writing.** Putting a thought into words is what makes it clear.

## Decisions

- **Message:** A, "thinking is made by writing" (chosen over "the second thought is yours" and "crafted, not generated").
- **AI in the mark:** usually left out, for the sake of minimalism. It goes in only if the result is unusually beautiful. Otherwise AI lives in the story, not the mark.
- **Letterform:** explore both: abstract marks, and marks built on a W for Writer.

## Who it's for and how it should feel

Writers, researchers, engineers who keep notes. The mark should feel calm, considered and quietly confident, like a well-made tool. It should not feel techy, sparkly, cute or loud.

## Visual rules

- Black and white only.
- One shape, two at most. Solid fills; negative space is fine.
- No outlines, stripes, gradients or textures.
- Geometric: squares, quarter circles, concave serif curves.
- Some asymmetry or rotational tension. The marks that felt alive so far had it.
- It must read at 16px and inside a macOS app-icon square.

## Signals from the explorations so far

- **Liked:** the Twist Cursor's two-serif gesture, the Nib, the Pilcrow, Echo's offset doubling, Strike, the calm of Still Water.
- **Disliked:** several parts in one mark; outlines and stripes; ideas forced together; metaphors far from writing (keys, lighthouses, hives); a meaning attached after the shape was drawn.

## Avoid

- The AI sparkle ✦, brains, lightbulbs, chat bubbles.
- A plain I-beam or serif "I". Lucide's icon set and Instapaper already use those.
- Sun-over-water marks, generic document or page icons, a pen inside a square.

## How we judge a mark

1. Its meaning fits in one sentence, and someone gets it without being told.
2. It's clearly about writing or thinking, not about technology.
3. It's distinct from marks that already exist.
4. Nothing can be removed from it.
5. It passes the 16px test.

## Concepts (round 1, no drawings)

Every concept answers "thinking is made by writing".

**Abstract**

1. **Point:** a circle with one square corner. Writing gives a thought its point.
2. **Edge:** a disc with one side cut dead straight. A vague thought gets a hard edge the moment it's written.
3. **Landing:** a drop shape, point up, sitting on a flat bottom. A thought lands on the line.
4. **Drawn Out:** a circle that stretches into a bar, round on the left and square on the right. The thought, drawn out into a line.
5. **Caret:** the proofreader's insertion mark. Put your own thought here.
6. **Whole Paragraph:** a block with a first-line indent and a short last line. A complete thought, start to finish.
7. **Carved Line:** a solid square with one short slit cut into it. One clear sentence carved out of a mass of thinking.
8. **Pilcrow:** ¶ as one geometric unit. The paragraph is the unit of a thought.
9. **Stroke:** a single diagonal pen stroke with concave serif ends, in the Twist style. The gesture of writing itself.
10. **Nib:** a nib reduced to a shield shape and a slit. Where a thought turns into ink.
11. **Untangle:** one loop, then a straight line. Writing is where the knot comes undone.
12. **Settle:** a tilted bar above a level one. A thought tilts until you write it down; then it's level.

**W for Writer**

13. **Caret W:** a W whose middle peak is the insertion caret. Writing is putting your thought in.
14. **Serif W:** a W drawn in strokes with concave serifs, in the Twist style. A calligrapher's W.
15. **Nib W:** a W whose two bottom points are pen nibs.
16. **Cup W:** two solid rounded cups side by side, built from quarter circles.
17. **Folded W:** a strip of paper folded twice into a W.
18. **Paragraph W:** the ragged right edge of four or five text lines traces a W. Risky at 16px.
19. **Stroke W:** one continuous pen line forming a W and ending in a full stop.
20. **Block W:** a solid square with two V-notches cut from the top. Risk: it may read as a crown.

## Process

1. **Concepts first, no drawings:** about 20 one-sentence ideas, each tied to the chosen message.
2. You pick about 5.
3. I draw 3 variations of each, minimal, following the rules above.
4. Narrow to 1, then refine the proportions.
